> **Documented version: 0.22.3.** The canonical user documentation, rendered inside
> WordPress at **Auctions → Social Calendar → Guide**. If you change a post type,
> rule, screen, setting, email or anything the plugin stores, update this file in
> the same commit (see `CLAUDE.md`). What the plugin is built to do is DJ's spec,

## 1. What this does

Intel Auctions Social turns each auction on intelauctions.com into a set of Facebook posts — and, once switched on, Google Business Profile posts — plans them three a day through OneUp, and keeps a person in the loop on the posts that carry hard facts. It is a separate plugin from the Intel Auctions Toolkit on purpose: it calls outside services on a schedule, and if something goes wrong, deactivating it on the Plugins page turns Facebook posting off without touching listings, schema or SEO.

It also takes each property's walkthrough video to YouTube (0.22.0): someone uploads the file once on the listing's row of **Property Videos**, and the plugin sends it to the Intel channel with a title and description written from the listing, puts the YouTube link on the listing, and uses the same file as the video in Facebook posts (§7, *Property videos to YouTube*).

It reads everything from the toolkit — the listing's fields, its stage, its open houses, its lead and their phone, its town and county, its sale price — so nothing is typed twice. **It needs the Intel Auctions Toolkit 4.66.0 or newer** (4.69.0 for the videos). Without it, it shows a notice at the top of the admin and does nothing at all.

## 3. Installing it

1. Make sure the **Intel Auctions Toolkit** is active at **4.66.0** or newer, with its **Anthropic API key** set (Intel SEO → Settings & Data → Services & Budgets from toolkit 4.67.0; Auctions → AI Content before it): every draft, photo grade and Talk it over answer is a Claude call through the toolkit, and without the key nothing is written (§6).
2. Plugins → Add New → Upload Plugin, choose `intel-auctions-social.zip` from the latest release at github.com/SixGricks/IntelAuctions-Social/releases, then **Activate**. A zip of the repository's `main` branch (Code → Download ZIP) installs too, as `IntelAuctions-Social-main`; updates keep whichever folder it is in. Updates then arrive through WordPress like any plugin's: the updater takes the latest release that is not a pre-release (its tag with or without a `v`), installs the release file named exactly `intel-auctions-social.zip`, and falls back to the release's source zip when there is none.
3. Paste the OneUp API key (from oneupapp.io → API access) into **Settings → OneUp → API key**. It is kept for this site only, never shown again or written to the log. Or define `INTEL_ONEUP_API_KEY` in `wp-config.php`, which wins over the one in Settings.
4. In **Settings**, turn on the master switch (with every post type still off, nothing posts) and save, then press **Look up accounts**: OneUp's categories and their accounts fill the lists. Choose the category and the Facebook page (for Intel: category **Intel**, page **Intel Auctions (Narvon)** — a category's only page is chosen for you), save, and press **Verify OneUp** (§7). Everything stays off until you turn it on.

If the toolkit is missing, too old, or failed to load part of itself, a yellow notice says **Intel Auctions Social is doing nothing** and why. No menu appears and nothing is planned until the toolkit is back.

## 4. The screens

Everything is under **Auctions → Social Calendar**, one menu item with five tabs: Calendar, Brand voice, Settings, Log and Guide. It stays in the menu in simple mode.

### Calendar

The next 30 days at a glance, in whole weeks, Monday to Sunday: the days of this week already gone are greyed and show what went out, and the days of the last week past the 30 are faint. Each post is a chip with its type, its time and the property, in its status's colour (the key above the grid lists the statuses on the calendar now; every status badge on the plugin's screens uses the same colours — DJ, Oct 14, 2026). Posts that will not go out are struck through. A post with no time yet sits on its best day, its time reading *no time yet*, with a dashed edge:

| Status | Colour | Means |
|---|---|---|
| Planned | Blue | In the plan, not yet sent anywhere |
| Waiting for approval | Purple | A roundup or Results post emailed for a yes (Lane A) |
| Approved | Teal | Someone said yes; it goes to OneUp on the next run |
| Pushed | Light green | On the OneUp calendar (posting on); its view shows the OneUp post id |
| Published | Intel navy, solid | Live on the Facebook page |
| Needs a decision | Yellow | Stopped for a person: a fair-housing phrase, a missing fact or a `[VERIFY]` marker in the draft, or the listing changed after a person edited the post |
| Failed | Orange | OneUp refused it three times in a row, or could not publish it — it waits for a person: **Try again** on its page, or skip it |
| Dropped | Red, struck through | A post that could be dropped had no room in its window (the reason is logged) |
| Skipped | Grey, struck through | A person turned it off (here — or by deleting it in OneUp, if anyone ever does) |
| Cancelled | Charcoal, struck through | The listing was unpublished or deleted, or Cancel posts was pressed |
| Human | Pink outline | A post a person scheduled for the page in OneUp; the plan works around it and never touches it |
| Not sent | Pale grey, dashed, struck through | A post whose time came in the old dry run (before 0.12.0): nothing went out. **Publish now** can still send it |

Sundays never carry a post, so their column is narrow. The hatched days are inside the 48-hour veto window, where nothing new is scheduled. Each day shows how many of its three places are taken (hover it: "2 of the 3 posts a day"). Today has a blue edge. On a phone the calendar is a list instead: **Today**, then each day that carries a post, with each run of empty days as one grey line ("Tue Oct 27 – Fri Oct 30 · nothing planned").

**Each post has a switch** (DJ, Oct 14, 2026: "I also want to be able to turn on skipped items and turn off others"), in its corner on the calendar: green when the post is on its way, grey when it is off. Press a green one to **turn the post off** (it is skipped: taken off OneUp at once if it was there, and it stays off); press a grey one — a post skipped, cancelled or dropped by the plan — to **turn it back on**: the plan gives it a time at once (or says it has no room in its window, to move by hand) and it goes to OneUp then. The page comes back to the calendar and says what happened. A post that went out, a person's own post, and one whose time came before posting was on have no switch. On a phone the switch is thumb-sized. A post's own page says the same: **Turn this post off…** (with a reason if you like) and **Turn it back on**.

**Hover a post for its preview** (DJ, Oct 14, 2026: "Hover over an item on the calendar should give a preview of the post"): after a moment, a card beside it shows its type and listing, where it stands and when it goes, its lead picture (and how many more), its words and the start of its first comment — or that it has no draft yet. Moving away, scrolling or Escape hides it. It costs nothing to show (the calendar carries it), and on a phone a tap simply opens the post.

**Drag a post to move it** (DJ, Oct 14, 2026: "drag posts around on the calendar and choose the morning, afternoon, or evening time slot"). On a computer, pick a post up and drop it on any day from today to 30 days out — or back on its own day — and choose **Morning** (6:00–7:30 am), **Afternoon** (11:30 am–1:00 pm) or **Evening** (7:00–9:00 pm), the posting windows from Settings; **Cancel** or Escape puts it back. The post takes its own minute in that window (the same few minutes past the start the plan gives it, never on the hour) and **stays there** like a Move: the plan works around it, and it goes to OneUp a moment later — inside the 48-hour window too, since moving it is your yes.

**A move moves only that post, and the page answers at once** (DJ, Oct 14, 2026: "Can it just move them and wait to run the agent until later. This will allow a human to make a bunch of changes without AI changing things at the same time"). No AI runs on a move; what took the time was the plan finding new times for the posts in the way and OneUp's round trips for each of them. Now:

- **Nothing else moves while you work.** The plan waits: the calendar says **The plan is waiting while you work** and how many posts you moved. Until it runs, a day can show more posts than it will keep, or two posts closer than three hours — the notice says so when a move fills a day.
- **The plan tidies up when you are done:** on the hourly run once nothing has moved for 15 minutes, or at once with **Plan now**. The posts you moved stay where you put them; the plan moves the others around them (or drops one that no longer fits its window, saying why on the post), and OneUp follows for every post it changed.
- **OneUp follows each move a moment after the page has answered** — the post is sent at the end of the same request, once your browser has its page. If that cannot happen (another run sending, or a host that cannot answer early), the open calendar's next check (within a minute) or the hourly run sends it.
- Restore, **Let the plan choose**, Try again and Plan now still plan straight away: they ask the plan for a time. Not on a Sunday (it takes no drop), not a window already gone today, and not into a window held by a post that stays put (published, a person's own, moved there, or on OneUp inside the 48 hours — posts are kept three hours apart): the notice says which post holds it. A post that went out, or one someone changed inside OneUp, cannot be dragged. On a phone, use the post's **Move…**.

**Nobody needs to open OneUp** (DJ, Oct 13, 2026: "make sure that Jacob and the intel team never needs to touch OneUp … instant update both directions"). Under the switches line, **OneUp: checked 20 seconds ago — nothing new** says when the plugin last heard from OneUp. While anyone has the calendar or a post open, it asks OneUp every minute what changed — one ask for everyone looking, so ten open screens cost what one does — and **Check now** asks at once. When OneUp has news (a post published, one it could not publish, one changed or deleted there), the line says so: the calendar shows it straight away; a post's page offers **Show the changes**, after asking if you have unsaved edits. OneUp sends nothing out by itself (it has no webhooks), so this asking is how "instant" works from OneUp's side; with nobody looking, the hourly run still reads everything back. The other way needs no asking: everything you change here goes to OneUp in the same click (§7, The pushes).

Click a post to open it. Under the title, one line says where it stands, and one line says whether anything can reach Facebook (the switches fold under it — **All the switches**): its status, when it goes out, its county and division, and the listing. Then:

- **A bar across the top** when the post waits for something — a decision (with the reasons, any `[VERIFY: …]` marker picked out in red), an approval, a rewrite after the listing changed, photos, or a person who skipped it — with the buttons that settle it right beside the reason. A post that waits for nothing has no bar.
- **Publish now**, in the bar, when the post's time has passed and it did not go out ("Its time (Sun Nov 1, 7:10 pm) has passed and it did not go out."): it goes a few minutes from now, to Facebook (and Google when Google is on for it), in the same click — on a roundup or Results post that is the approval too. While posting is off for it the bar says why instead ("Publish now: not while Listing posts are off"). See §7, The pushes.
- **Edit** (left): the post text and the first comment, full width, and **Save changes**. Save changes is greyed out until something on the post changes — except on a post that stopped for a decision, where saving it as it is checks it again against today's listing and settings; once anything is changed and not saved — the words, the comment, the pictures, their alt text — every Save changes turns blue and says **Not saved yet** beside it. On a roundup or Results post the bar's **Approve** then waits ("Save your changes first") — it approves the saved post, not what is on the screen — and **Save and approve** appears beside Save changes.
- **Talk it over** (left, under Edit): a chat with Claude about this post — see below.
- **Pictures** (left, under Edit): the post's pictures in posting order, square, each with its alt text underneath; the first one is marked **Lead**. Drag a picture to move it (or use ‹ ›), × takes it out of this post (not out of the Media Library), **+ Add pictures** opens the Media Library — any picture there, not only the listing's gallery. **✎** on a picture opens its alt text in the tile (what a screen reader says for it; Enter or **Done** keeps it, Escape cancels, blank goes back to the plugin's). On a phone ‹ › always show, and a finger can drag a picture too. **Save changes** here saves the pictures together with everything else changed on the post; a post carries at most 10. Pictures chosen by hand hold: a rewrite, or the listing changing, keeps them, and **Let the plugin choose again** hands the choice back. A roundup or Results post needs approving again with new pictures (not for alt text alone). A person's alt text holds through rewrites too. Leaving the page with anything unsaved asks first.
- **Preview** (middle): the post as Facebook shows it — the page's name and time, the words with any `[VERIFY]` marked, up to five pictures as Facebook lays them out, and the first comment as a comment. With Google on, or a preview of Instagram, X or LinkedIn (§7), a tab for each shows the post as that network gets it. The **Google Business Profile** tab shows its picture, words and **Learn more** button, where it stands on Google (on OneUp for when, published, kept off and why, or not sent and why), and **Keep this one off Google** — which takes its Google copy off at once and leaves the Facebook post as it is — or, on a post kept off, **Send it to Google again**.
- **About this post**, **What goes to OneUp** (the request for Facebook and Google, folded, and what stops it if anything does), **Approval** for a roundup or Results post, and **History** (newest first, with people's names) on the right.

On a wide screen these are three columns; on a laptop, two (the preview over the details); on a phone, one, with the preview first. The preview follows the words as they are typed — or as Claude changes them — before anything is saved. From the bar and the Edit card:

- **Save changes** — save everything changed on the post: the words, the first comment, the pictures and their alt text, all at once. **It never approves the post.** The words are checked again like a fresh draft, the first comment for fair housing, `[VERIFY]` and the listing link (a comment that lost the link stops for a decision), and saving words counts as checking them against the listing as it is now. Pictures or alt text changed on their own leave the words as Claude wrote them. Once a person has edited a post, the plugin never rewrites it behind their back: if the listing changes afterwards, the post stops (**Needs a decision**, even if it was already approved) until you save it again, keep it as written, or press **Rewrite**.
- **Rewrite** — have Claude write it again (at most once an hour per post; it counts against the monthly Claude cap). **Write it now** does the same for a post with no draft yet.
- **Turn this post off…** (Skip) — opens to take it off the plan, with a reason if you like; it comes off OneUp at once. It stays off until you press **Turn it back on** (Restore) — or flip its switch on the calendar — and the plan then gives it a time at once. Turning it back on also overrides a skip the plugin made itself (say, "Facebook already got the full listing from the webhook"): it stays on the plan, with a week from today to go out.
- **Keep as written** — for a post that stopped for a decision: you have read why (including "the listing changed"), and it is right as it is. Not for a `[VERIFY: …]` marker: Claude left it where a fact was missing, and a marked fact never goes out as it is — put the fact in its place and save, or fix the listing and let the post be rewritten. A post that stopped stays stopped until someone decides — being dropped and brought back, restored, or called for again never turns it into Planned; and a later edit that stops it again needs a new decision.
- For a roundup or Results post: **Approve**, **Send for approval now** (before the hourly run would), **Send a reminder now**, and **Save and approve** beside Save changes (it does both). Save changes on its own never approves: on a post waiting for a yes it saves, voids the old approval links, and sends the new version for its yes — one email for everything saved at once. Its **Approval** box shows who was emailed when and who said yes.
- **Move…** (under **Goes out**, in About this post): a day, and **Morning**, **Afternoon** or **Evening** — or **At** an exact time — and **Move it**. A time of day works as a drag on the calendar does: the post's own minute in that window. The post goes out then and stays there — the plan works around it when it tidies up (above) — and the move reaches OneUp a moment after the page answers, even inside the 48-hour window (you moved it: that is the yes). Not on a Sunday, not in the next 15 minutes, not more than 30 days out; a day that already has three posts is pointed out. **Let the plan choose the time** hands it back (a post already on OneUp and inside the 48-hour window stays where it is). A dropped post you move comes back on the plan.
- **Try again** on a post OneUp refused or could not publish: back on the plan, and it goes to OneUp at once (at its time if that is still ahead, else the plan gives it the next one — Move for another). **OneUp's own failed copy is rescheduled** for that time (DJ, Oct 14, 2026) — the same post on OneUp, no second copy and nothing left on OneUp's failed list — when the post is as it was when it failed. If it changed since, OneUp will not reschedule it, or it went to OneUp before 0.19.0, a fresh copy goes instead and the failed one stays on OneUp's failed list, never taken for this one. An answer that never comes back is looked for on OneUp's list before anything is sent again; and while OneUp still lists the old failure beside the rescheduled copy, the post is not failed again.
- A post on OneUp shows its **On OneUp** box: its OneUp post id and time, and whether a person moved or edited it there. A post a person changed in OneUp is kept as they made it — until someone presses **Edit it here again** in its bar: their version (words and time) becomes the post's, nothing is re-sent, and from then on it is edited here, every save reaching OneUp at once. On the Google tab, **Send this version to Google instead** does the same for a Google copy changed in OneUp.

#### Talk it over

Say what you don't like about the post — "shorter", "less salesy", "lead with the aerial", "say View full listing in the comment", "better alt text on the first picture" — and Claude changes the post on your screen there and then: **the text, the first comment, which pictures go with it and in what order, and their alt text**. The preview follows. Press Enter (or **Send**) to send; the chips under the chat (**Shorter**, **Plainer — less salesy**, **Warmer**, **Lead with the auction date**, **Fix grammar only**) send themselves. Claude reads the post as it stands on your screen, your own unsaved edits included, and the conversation so far.

- **Pictures** come only from the post's own and the property's photos, which Claude is shown with their kind, grade and alt text (and whether another post of the property already uses one). Claude cannot see the photos themselves, so its alt text rewords what grading saw.
- What Claude cannot change — when the post goes out, its type, the listing's facts — it says, and where to change it (facts on the listing itself).
- **Nothing is saved until you press Save changes**, in the Edit card, in the **Not saved yet** strip that appears in the chat, or under the pictures. Saving never approves. Then everything is checked like any edit (bidding, figures, fair housing, `[VERIFY]`, the listing link in the comment), the words become a person's edit, and a roundup or Results post needs a fresh yes. The history notes it was talked over with Claude. **Discard changes** throws away what is not saved.
- **Each answer shows what it changed**, right in the chat: the words taken out (struck through) and put in (underlined) in the text or the comment, and the new pictures as small thumbnails, the lead first. **Show me** scrolls to the changed field and flashes it. Under each answer a note keeps track: **On the post above — not saved yet**, **Saved**, or **Not on the post now** (undone, or replaced by a later version) — with **Use this version** to bring it back.
- **Undo the last change** puts back only what Claude's last change changed. If you typed in that same field since, it asks first.
- When a version would stop for a decision if saved (a fair-housing phrase, a wrong bidding method, a figure the listing doesn't have, a first comment without the listing link, no pictures at all), Claude's answer says so under it — before you save.
- Claude keeps to the same rules as a draft: the listing's facts, how bidding works, fair housing, the house rules. It will use a fact you give it that the listing lacks (and say the listing doesn't have it, so it can be fixed there too), but never one of its own, and not one the listing contradicts. It keeps the post's shape (§6) — bullets stay bullets, an emoji-led post keeps its emoji lines — unless you ask for another.
- When you ask for something that should hold for every post ("never say nestled"), the answer offers **Add to the house voice** — one click puts it in the house rules (Brand voice, below).
- **Start over** clears the conversation on screen; what you asked for stays with the post, a **Rewrite** of the post is told it, and Brand voice keeps it.
- Each message is one Claude call against the monthly cap. A post gets at most **30 answers** (Settings → Talk it over; 0 takes the chat away); a double click is answered once, and one message at a time is answered per post. Without Claude (no key, or the cap spent) the chat is there but cannot send, and says why. A post that has gone to OneUp, or that a person changed in OneUp, has no chat.

Above the grid, when the plan last ran and what it did; beside **Pause**, **Plan now**. Then, only when there is any, what waits on a person: **Needs a decision** (drafts that stopped and posts that failed, with why), **Waiting for approval** (roundup and Results posts emailed for a yes, how long ago and how many emails) and **Corrections to post as a comment** (§7). Under the grid: **Drafts** (how many planned posts have no draft yet, and **Write drafts now**, which writes up to four on the spot), **Waiting for photos** (Upcoming listings whose Listing post waits for a gallery), **Dropped in the last 30 days** (with each reason) and **Running late or squeezed** — one line when there are none.

Across the top of every screen, the **switches strip** shows the master switch, pause, each post type and Google when it is on — so it is always plain whether anything can reach Facebook or Google. Every switch is **on or off**: on, it really posts; off, nothing of it goes. On the calendar and a post's page it is one line — "On: Listing posts go to Facebook.", "Off: …", "Paused: …", or "Not posting yet: Verify OneUp has not passed for this page." when a switch is on but something it needs is missing — and one for Google, with **All the switches** under it. (Instagram, X and LinkedIn are previews, not switches, and are not listed.)

**Pause all posting** on the calendar turns everything off for now without deactivating the plugin: nothing new goes, and posts on OneUp that have not gone out yet are **taken back off** — at once, soonest first (the rest within the hour) — and go again when you press **Resume posting**, even inside the 48-hour window. Planning, drafting and the read-back carry on while paused, and Facebook stays with this plugin (a pause must not let the toolkit's webhook post what this plugin posts on resume).

**Posts past their time.** When a post's time has passed without it going out — posting was off, it waited for a yes, or its time came in the old dry run — the calendar lists it ("passed its time without going out", the last 7 days) and its page offers **Publish now** (§4, Calendar; §7, The pushes).

### Brand voice

How Claude learns to sound like Intel. **The house rules are what lasts**; the log of posts is the raw material they are learned from, kept for a while and then forgotten. In three parts:

- **House rules** — short rules about style, one per line, up to 30: "Never use the word nestled.", "Open with the place, not with us." They go into every draft and every Talk it over answer, after the rules on facts, bidding and fair housing, which they never overrule. A rule that would put a fair-housing phrase in a post is not kept. They are a person's to write: type them here, or add one from the chat.
- **The log of final posts** — every post a person saved or approved, and every post that went out, for **120 days** — a starred post for good: whose words they are ("Edited by …", "Approved as written by …", "As Claude wrote it"), what was changed (open it for Claude's draft with the words taken out struck through and the words put in underlined) and what was asked for in Talk it over. Filter it to the posts edited, approved, gone out as Claude wrote them, starred, or not learned from.
- **Examples** — the newest posts a person wrote, approved or starred are shown to Claude in each new draft as how the team sounds (Settings → Examples in each draft: 3; 0 for none), one of them as Claude's draft beside what the team changed it to. Never a post about the same listing, never one that stopped for a decision, never one with a `[VERIFY]` marker, and Claude is told never to take a fact or phrase from them.

**The voice is learned only from what a person wrote or said yes to.** A post that went out as Claude wrote it is in the log but teaches nothing — unless you press **Star as a good example**. **Don't learn from this** takes any post out of the examples and out of Suggest updates.

**Suggest updates from the log** has Claude read the newest posts that teach — what was changed and what was asked for — and suggest house rules. The suggestion appears above the rules with what it would add and take out, and how many ("Adds 1 rule and takes out 3 of your 4"), in a box you can edit; nothing changes until you press **Use these rules** — called **Replace my rules with these** when it would take any out — or **Discard the suggestion**. When there is nothing to read yet, the button is greyed out and says why. It is one Claude call; pressed again within 10 minutes with nothing new in the log, it says so instead of paying twice.

**It also learns on its own** (Settings → Learn on its own, on at install): once **8** new posts teach and Claude last read the log a week or more ago, the hourly job has it read the log and suggest rules — one call a week at most, never while its last suggestion still waits. The suggestion waits for a person the same way, and the calendar and the weekly email say it is there, with the rules it would add and take out.

**Kept small.** Once a day the log forgets posts older than 120 days (never a starred one) and the oldest past 400; Talk it over's messages go after 45 days (what was asked is already on the post's row in the log), and a person's message keeps only a fingerprint of the words it was about, not a second copy. A busy year comes to a few megabytes, and what Claude is sent never grows with it: at most 3 example posts per draft, 30 rules, and 15 posts per read of the log.

## 5. Post types

From the build spec. Weight sets priority; a **guaranteed** post can slide within its window but is never dropped; **Lane A** posts wait for an emailed yes, **Lane B** posts are scheduled with a 48-hour window to veto them.

| Post | When | Window | Weight | Guaranteed | Lane |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| New This Week roundup | Fridays, when any listing entered Recently Announced that week | Fri → Fri → Sat | 60 | Yes, when there are new listings | A |
| Listing Posted | The listing moves to Upcoming | promotion day → same day → +7 days | 70 | Yes | B |
| Open House | Each open house | 7 days before → 5 → 3 | 90 | No | B |
| Auction Week | The sale | 10 days before → 7 → 3 | 80 | No | B |
| Results | The listing moves to Past with a sale price | next day → next day → whenever approved | 100 | Yes | A |
| Human post | A person posts directly | — | outside scoring | Never moved | — |

Starting-bid multipliers: $500,000 and up ×1.2; $250,000–500,000 ×1.1; $100,000–250,000 ×1.0; under $100,000 or none ×0.9.

## 6. How posts are written

### Which posts each listing gets

The plugin checks every hour which posts the listings call for (the table in §5) and keeps the Social Calendar in step:

- A listing in **Recently Announced** gets no post of its own; it goes into that Friday's **New This Week** roundup (announced from the Friday before through Thursday). Listings already posted in full to Facebook by the toolkit's webhook before this plugin took Facebook over don't get a second Listing post — theirs shows as skipped, with the date.
- Moving to **Upcoming** (Publish Upcoming) starts the **Listing** post's window that day — or the day photos arrive, if they come later. A listing that is already Upcoming when the plugin first sees it counts from that day. With no photos yet, the Listing post waits (under **Waiting for photos**). If the photos still aren't there when its auction week starts, the Listing post is skipped and the **Auction Week** post runs anyway, guaranteed, with the brochure cover.
- Each **open house** day gets its post 7 → 5 → 3 days before (never before the listing went Upcoming) — two sessions on the same day share it, and it gives both times; the **sale** gets its Auction Week post 10 → 7 → 3 days before. Two or more listings selling the **same day at the same place** (the same town — the Sunbury twins, both Lila Lane duplexes, 393 Boyer Rd and its contents) share one Auction Week post and one Results post. Two unrelated sales that only share a date keep their own posts.
- **Results** come the day after the sale once it is in Past with a price (a Saturday sale's on the Monday), and wait as long as it takes for a yes — a Results post on the plan never expires. A liquidation needs no price: its post is a thank-you for the turnout. Installing the plugin does not dig up old results — only sales from a week before it arrived on.
- A listing that is unpublished, made private, password-protected, trashed or deleted loses its posts **at once** — before the page that did it has finished loading — and a shared post simply loses that listing; republish it and they come back. **Cancel posts** on the listing panel does the same on purpose (for the rare sale that is called off); **Resume posts** undoes it. A post that is no longer called for (a date or an open house changed) is cancelled. What a person decided — skipped, restored, cancelled, edited — is never undone. A post already sent is never changed, and neither is an approved one, except that losing a listing or a fact behind it costs it its approval (§8): a listing that joins its sale day afterwards gets its own post.
- A listing's **Open House** and **Auction Week** posts never go before its Listing post: while the Listing post waits for photos they wait too (saying so), and they are planned once the photos arrive — or on their own once the Listing post is skipped.

Each post has a window and an ideal day (a Sunday ideal moves to Monday, or Saturday when Monday is past the window); the morning plan (below) gives each one its day and minute.

### The words

Claude writes every post through the toolkit's Claude engine, from the listing's facts and nothing else: the address, town and county from the geocoded listing (the county is left out rather than guessed), the auction date and time (a sale with a date and no time gives no time — the site's date-only field reads as midnight, and Claude is never told "12:00 am") and how bidding works, the starting bid and size, the open houses with their times and lengths, the sellers, the lead and their direct phone from the team record, and the sale price from the results. Every prompt forbids inventing anything — a missing fact comes back as a `[VERIFY: …]` marker, which stops the post.

On top of the post type's brief go:

- **one of five openings**, rotating: place first, date first, person first, question first, numbers first. **Results posts have five of their own**: result first (the property and the price — skipped when there is no price), thanks first (the sellers, by name only as the listing gives them), place first, sale day first, and to the neighbors (the town or county). Never the opening this property's last post used, and the least used this month first;
- **a shape**, rotating, so the posts don't all look alike (DJ, Oct 13, 2026: "sometimes paragraphs and sometimes bullets… 1 out of every 5 being emoji heavy"): **paragraphs** (two or three short ones), **bullets** (one lead-in sentence, then 3 to 5 short "• " lines of facts), **short** (two or three sentences — Auction Week and Results only), and **emoji-led** (the facts on short lines, each starting with one fitting emoji — 📍 the place, 📅 the date, 💲 the price — up to 8 in all). Paragraphs come up most, then bullets, and emoji-led about one post in five; the roundup is its plain list, emoji-led now and then. The least used shape for the post type in the last 45 days goes next (weighted), never the shape this property's last post used. The post's page says which opening and shape it was written as;
- **the division's voice**: Farm & Home, Investment & Recreation, or Intel general (liquidations, equipment), from the listing's Auction Category;
- **a call to action**, rotating: call the lead, join the email or call list, comment with a question, tag someone who'd want it, reply for the brochure, follow the page, save the date, share it with someone who has had an eye on a place like it, thinking of selling? (call the office), and a thank-you to the sellers and everyone who came out — each where it fits the post type. **Results rotates through six**: join the list, a question, thinking of selling, follow the page, share it, and the thank-you (a liquidation's thank-you keeps to the list, follow, selling and thank-you);
- **the house style**: plainspoken and local, no stock marketing gloss, at most one emoji (an emoji-led post excepted), never two exclamation points in a row, no links, no hashtags — and **Intel Phone Bidding said once when the sale is a phone-bid one**, in positive words ("bid by phone through Intel Phone Bidding"). An in-person auction is the usual kind and **need not be said at all** (DJ, Oct 14, 2026: "We only need to say when it's a phone bid auction"); "on site" is fine where it reads naturally. Never what a sale isn't ("no onsite auction", "not online"), never both, never online;
- **no open house set: "Call for a private showing" — sometimes** (DJ, Oct 14, 2026: "we don't always need to say that"). A Listing or Auction Week post about one property, real estate, with no open house still ahead says it in about half the posts — the post itself decides, the same way every time it is rewritten — and the rest leave showings out. An open house is never invented;
- **the house rules and examples** from Brand voice (§4): the team's own rules after the ones above, and the newest posts a person wrote or approved, as how the team sounds;
- **what the team asked for on this post** in Talk it over, when the post is rewritten.

**No opening line, call to action or hashtag set repeats within 30 days.** The plugin keeps every one it has used (`{prefix}ias_history`), hands Claude the recent ones to avoid, and asks once more if one repeats anyway; a second repeat stops the post for a decision. Hashtags are chosen by the plugin, not Claude: `#IntelAuctions` plus two of the town, the county, the post type and the division, in a combination not used in 30 days.

The post is the opening line, the body, the call to action and the hashtags. **The listing link goes in the first comment**, after a lead-in from Settings → First comment ("Photos, terms and the full listing:" for a Listing or Auction Week post, "Photos, directions and the full listing:" for an Open House, "View the full listing:" for Results — DJ: never "how it sold" — "Photos, terms and details:" for a post about several properties, "Every new sale, with dates as they are set:" for the roundup), tagged `utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=social_<post type>&utm_content=p<post id>`; a shared post lists each property's link, the roundup links to the Recently Announced page, and a listing with a video link (YouTube, Vimeo, Facebook) gets it in the comment too. With **Listing link** unticked in Settings, the link ends the post instead.

### The pictures

- **Photo grading.** The first time a listing's photos are needed, Claude looks at each one once — in batches of 8, at the small grading size — and scores it 1–10 for a Facebook post, tags its kind (aerial, exterior, interior, barn or outbuilding, land, other) and writes alt text from what it can see. The grade is kept on the photo, so it is never paid for twice. The first 40 photos of a gallery are graded.
- **Choosing.** Only photos Claude has graded are chosen once a gallery is partly graded (at least six, or all of a small one), so every picture comes with its kind and alt text known. **Never the same view twice in one post:** every photo gets a fingerprint (a tiny grey thumbnail, worked out once), and two shots of the same view — the house front from a step to the left — never share a post; an Open House post also skips the views the Listing post used. Every post about a property leads with a photo that property hasn't used yet — nobody sees the same aerial twice. The Listing post mixes kinds, leading with the best aerial or exterior and following it with a different kind, and takes up to 10 while leaving enough for the property's later posts (3 per open house, 2 for auction week). Open House posts favour interiors and use none of the Listing post's photos (3–5; a small gallery fills out with the Listing post's rather than posting one picture). Auction Week posts lead with the strongest aerial not yet used. A shared post takes one or two of each property's. Photos scoring 3 or less are left out while better ones exist.
- **Video.** When the property has a video file — uploaded on Property Videos (0.22.0, kept on OneUp's storage), or a listing whose video field is itself a file (mp4, mov) — the Open House post is that video, played on Facebook itself. A YouTube, Vimeo or Facebook link goes in the Listing post's first comment ("Video walkthrough: …") — and in an Open House post's when it has no file — so Facebook sends people to the channel too.
- **Three crops.** Every chosen photo is saved as landscape 1200×628, square 1080×1080 and vertical 1080×1350 (never enlarged past the photo), under `uploads/intel-social/crops/`, ready for Instagram later.
- **Chosen by hand.** A person can choose any post's pictures on its page (§4); their choice then holds through rewrites.
- **Alt text** on every picture: from grading, else the photo's own alt text, else the listing's name; the plugin's own graphics describe themselves ("Sold for $425,000: 22 Mill Road, Paradise, PA, October 31, 2026").

### The pictures the plugin draws

- **Results graphic** — the featured photo, a dark gradient up from the bottom, "SOLD" and the price in white, the street, and the town and date. Two or three same-day sales share one picture side by side, one price each. A liquidation never gets one: its thank-you carries a plain photo.
- **New This Week collage** — the week's brochure covers on Intel navy under a "NEW THIS WEEK" heading; a listing with no cover yet gets a branded card (street, town, date) in its place. Up to nine; more shows "+N more".
- The font is the **brand font** set in Settings (a .ttf or .otf in the Media Library), else the bundled **Inter** (SIL Open Font License, `assets/fonts/OFL.txt`). The pictures are 1080×1080 JPEGs in `uploads/intel-social/graphics/`.

### The checks before anything goes anywhere

Every draft — written by Claude or edited by a person — is checked, and stops for a decision (**Needs a decision**, with the reasons) when:

- the listing changed after a person edited the post, or while the draft was already waiting for a decision (a draft nobody touched is simply rewritten on the next run, and goes nowhere until it is);
- it carries a `[VERIFY]` marker;
- a **phone-bid** sale's Listing, Open House or Auction Week post doesn't say it is Intel Phone Bidding, or says in-person too (buyers would think they can choose); an **in-person** sale's post mentions phone bidding; or the listing itself doesn't say which it is — its own Auction type field and its Auction Type category are read, and if they disagree the post stops. An in-person post that never says "in-person" is fine. Any post mentioning online bidding stops. A way of bidding the sentence **denies** is not a mention of it: "Intel Phone Bidding — no onsite auction" is a phone post, not one that says both (DJ, Oct 13, 2026: that was held, and it was too aggressive), and "no online bidding" is not a mention of online bidding. The drafts are asked for positive words anyway;
- it gives a dollar figure that isn't on the listing (starting bid, sale price, parcel bids, or its own description) or a phone number that isn't the lead's or the office's;
- the **fair-housing screen** finds language about who should live there rather than the property: "perfect for families", "family-friendly", "safe neighborhood", "great for retirees", "adults only", a religion, nationality or ability, pointing to a house of worship, "bachelor pad" — plus any phrases added in Settings. Describing the property (a white barn, Amish-built, a wheelchair ramp, a family room) is never flagged;
- it has more than three emoji — or more than eight on an emoji-led post.

Two things are fixed without asking: a web address in the text is removed (the link belongs in the first comment), and stacked exclamation points become one.

### When drafts are written, and what they cost

The plugin's job runs every hour on the toolkit's dispatcher (`ias_social`, listed with the toolkit's jobs): it updates the plan, then writes up to **8 drafts** (Settings), approve-first posts (Results, the roundup) first and then by day, for the next 30 days. The roundup is written on its Friday, once the week's announcements are in. Each draft is one Claude call (two if the first answer was unusable or repeated itself), each photo batch another, all counted against the toolkit's monthly Claude cap — when the cap is reached, drafting stops until next month or until the cap is raised.

Nothing is paid for twice: a draft is rewritten only when the listing's facts behind it change (and nobody has edited it) — on the next run, at most once an hour — or when a person presses **Rewrite** (at most hourly). A failed call waits 6 hours before trying again. Until a draft whose facts changed has been rewritten, it is never pushed.

**Talk it over** costs one call per message (the post, its facts and the conversation so far: a few thousand tokens), at most 30 a post; **Suggest updates** one call per press, and Learn on its own one a week at most. The examples add roughly 250 tokens per example to each draft. All of it is counted against the same monthly cap, under its own feature name in the toolkit's usage (`social.chat`, `social.voice`).

**Without Claude** — the toolkit has no Anthropic key, or the month's cap is spent — nothing is tried: no draft, no photo grade, no post marked failed. A notice under the switches on every Social screen says **Drafts are stopped** and why, and **Settings → Claude** shows it too, with the month's tokens against the cap. The plan carries on (posts without words are never pushed), and the next hourly run after the key is back, or the cap raised, writes the drafts.

## 7. The plan

### The morning run

Every morning from **5:00** (Settings: the planning hour) the plugin's hourly job re-plans the next **30 days**. **Plan now** on the calendar runs the same plan on the spot. In order:

1. **What is fixed stays put.** A post already published, a human post, a post someone edited in OneUp, a post a person moved to its time (Move or a drag on the calendar, while that time is ahead), and a post already on the OneUp calendar inside the 48-hour veto window are never moved or dropped; the plan works around them (the hourly read-back from OneUp keeps them current).
2. **Cancelled listings are already gone** — cancelling happens the moment a listing is unpublished or deleted (§6).
3. **Every other post goes on its ideal day** if it can. **Guaranteed posts first** (Listing, Results, the roundup), by priority; then the rest by priority.
4. **A full day** (three posts) pushes the newcomer to the nearest day in its window with room. A post that finds no room anywhere makes a lower one move: a guaranteed post can move any Open House or Auction Week post, and a higher-priority Open House or Auction Week post can move a lower one. The post that made way looks again elsewhere in its own window.
5. **A droppable post with no room anywhere in its window is dropped**, with the reason, under **Dropped in the last 30 days** on the calendar and in the log. A guaranteed post is never dropped: with no room in its window it **runs late** (up to 14 days after it, but a Listing post never later than the day before its sale), and only if even that fails is the same-county rule bent for it — both are listed under **Running late or squeezed**.
6. **A day with fewer than two posts** pulls in the best post whose window reaches it — a dropped one first, else one moved from a day with three. (Blog fill-ins join here once blogs resume.)
7. **The sense rules hold throughout**: one post per property per day, no two posts about the same county on one day, a property's Open House and Auction Week posts after its Listing post (and not at all while the Listing post waits for photos), and when two days are equally good, the one without that division already.
8. **The pushes** (below).

A post keeps the day and minute it already has whenever they still work, so the calendar — and later OneUp — only changes when something forces it. A dropped post comes back by itself if room opens up. A post the plan can't place at all loses its old time, saying why, so nothing goes out at a time the plan no longer holds.

### Times

- **Never on Sunday.** Monday to Saturday, at most **3 posts a day**, at least **3 hours apart**.
- In the posting windows **6:00–7:30 AM, 11:30 AM–1:00 PM and 7:00–9:00 PM**: the day's highest-priority post takes the evening, the next midday, the third the morning (working around any fixed post's time).
- Each post's time is shifted by **up to 25 minutes** from the window's start — the same shift for the same post every day — and never lands on the hour.
- **Nothing new closer than 48 hours** for Listing, Open House and Auction Week posts (Lane B): that is the window to skip, move or change it on the calendar. A post a person moved there themselves is the exception — they said yes. A roundup or Results post (Lane A) is planned at least an hour ahead, because it goes nowhere until someone approves it; once approved, it takes the next free time.

### The pushes

After planning, every post scheduled in the next **21 days** that is ready — a Lane B post with a draft that passed its checks, or an approved Lane A post — is turned into the exact request for OneUp's API, and every post that went out and is no longer ready — dropped, skipped, cancelled, stopped for a decision, its listing changed, its approval withdrawn, or moved past the 21 days — is turned into the request that takes it back:

- A Listing, Open House or Auction Week post that has not gone out yet goes only while its time is still at least **48 hours** away (it can be released late, say by Keep as written on a Monday afternoon for a Wednesday morning): otherwise it waits, and the next morning's plan gives it a time outside the veto window. A post already on OneUp whose words change (a corrected fact, an edit) is replaced whenever it changes, so it never goes out with the old facts.
- Every call follows OneUp's API documentation (docs.oneupapp.io, read Oct 14, 2026). A post is sent by **POST** (a method OneUp refuses is tried once the other way). A post with pictures is `scheduleimagepost` (up to 10 pictures, their addresses joined by `~~`), a video `schedulevideopost`, words alone `scheduletextpost`, each with the OneUp category id, the Facebook account id — sent as a list of one, as OneUp requires ("Social Network ID must be an array"): `["1336677789520495"]`, or `social_network_id[0]=…` if OneUp refuses the first with those words (the form that works is kept, `ias_oneup_id_form`; a refusal creates nothing, so the second try can never make a second post) — the time as `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM` (site time — OneUp's account time zone must match; Verify OneUp checks it), the text, and the tagged link as `first_comment`.
- OneUp's answer to a new post is "1 new Posts Scheduled." with no post id, so the post is found on OneUp's scheduled list by its words and time, and its id kept from then on. "0 new Posts Scheduled." is a refusal: nothing was created.
- **OneUp's lists come 50 posts a page.** The plugin reads every page — the next from where the last ended, until a page comes back short (or OneUp says where the next one starts) — so a post of ours past the first 50 is still found, kept and taken back by its id. A list that does not move past its first page is never treated as the whole list, and nothing is concluded from it (a post is only ever taken for deleted in OneUp on a whole list).
- **Gone out.** OneUp's published list may give Facebook's (or Google's) own id for a post rather than the one it was scheduled under. A post there whose id is not one of ours is matched by its words, on our page (or Google location), at its time (from 15 minutes before it to a day after): it is confirmed as **Published**, the network's id is kept with it, and it is never taken for a person's post. Only a status is read this way — nothing is ever taken off or replaced by a match of words. A person's own published post is still recognised as theirs.
- A post OneUp could not publish shows OneUp's own reason (`fail_reason`).
- A moved or changed post is deleted and scheduled again (OneUp cannot change a scheduled post's time; its `editpost` changes only the words). Live posts are never edited.
- The API key (`INTEL_ONEUP_API_KEY`) is added only when sending and never appears in the log or on screen.

**A person's change goes at once.** Save changes, Rewrite, Skip, Restore, Keep as written, Let the plan choose, Try again, Edit it here again and Google's own buttons send that post to OneUp in the same click — and when the click re-plans (Restore, Let the plan choose, Try again), every other post the plan moved, dropped or brought back goes to OneUp in that click too (0.19.1). Move and a drag send their post a moment after the page has answered, and do not re-plan (0.20.0, above) — scheduled, replaced or taken back, for Facebook and Google — under the same rules as the hourly run, and the page says what happened ("OneUp — Facebook: replaced on OneUp for Thu Oct 15, 12:10 pm."). An approval already went at once. First the plugin reads OneUp's scheduled list, so a change a person made there is still never overwritten; that quick read never concludes that a post was deleted — only a full read-back can (two of them half an hour apart). If another run is sending at that moment, the change follows on the next one, and the page says so. With posting off and nothing of the post on OneUp, there is nothing to send and the page says nothing about OneUp.

**On or off — there is no dry run** (DJ, Oct 14, 2026: "I want to turn dry run off and remove it. Either things are active or inactive."). A post goes to OneUp only while **all** of these hold: the master switch on, nothing paused, its post type on, the key and the ids set, and **Verify OneUp passed for that page and key** (set another category, account or key and it must pass again; this is Facebook — Google has its own switch and Verify, below). A Results post or roundup waiting for approval never goes. Each post's view shows **What goes to OneUp**: the exact request, and what stops it if anything does.

- **Off means off.** When the master switch, Pause or a post's type is off, nothing new goes, and **a post already on OneUp that has not gone out yet is taken back off** — at once when the switch is saved (soonest first; what does not fit in the page's time follows on the next hourly run), and on every run after. It goes again when the switch is on, even inside the 48-hour window (it was on OneUp before). With the master switch off and nothing of ours left on OneUp, the plugin makes no call at all. A missing key, a missing id or Verify not yet passed stops anything new but takes nothing back — those are not a person switching something off. A post a person changed inside OneUp itself is kept as they made it; **Skip** takes it off.
- **On.** A post is **Pushed** once OneUp has it; its OneUp post id is kept, and the calendar shows it. If OneUp refuses a post, it is tried again on the next hourly runs; the third refusal in a row makes it **Failed**, waiting for a person (**Try again**). A cancelled listing's posts come off OneUp **at once**, before the page that cancelled them has finished.
- **The update to 0.12.0** retired the dry run once: on a site that was in it, every post type and Google were switched off, so the update never started posting by itself, and a note under the switches says what was switched off until Settings is next saved. A site already live kept its switches. The dry run's records went with it; posts whose time came in it show as **Not sent**.
- While posts are coming back off OneUp, the calendar says how many and which ("coming back off OneUp"); without the OneUp key nothing can be taken back, and it says to put the key back.

**Publish now.** A post whose time has passed without it going out — posting was off at its time, it waited for a yes, or its time came in the old dry run — gets **Publish now** on its page (DJ, Oct 14, 2026). It goes to Facebook (and Google a minute later, when Google is on for it) a few minutes ahead — 3 — in the same click, and that time holds: the morning plan works around it. On a roundup or Results post, pressing it is the approval (recorded as "Publish now"). It is offered only when the post could go: never while posting is off for it (the master switch, Pause, its type, Verify OneUp — its page says which instead of the button), never on a Sunday, and not on a post that needs a decision, has a `[VERIFY]` marker or is waiting to be rewritten. Left alone, the morning plan gives such a post a new time if it still has a window.

### Corrections

A post that has gone out is never edited — editing resets its reach. When a fact it carried changes afterwards (the auction date or time, the starting bid, the sale price, the address, the phone number, an open house), the plugin drafts the correction — "Update: the starting bid is now $275,000 (not $300,000)." — and lists it under **Corrections to post as a comment** on the calendar, on the post's page and in the Monday email.

- **Post it as a comment** posts it on the Facebook post now, as the page, through OneUp's comments (its Intermediate plan or above; DJ, Oct 14, 2026). The post's page then says who posted it. If you posted it yourself on Facebook, press **I posted it**; if it is not needed, **Not needed**.
- **Post corrections automatically** (Settings → OneUp, **Correction comments**; **off at install**, because it is public): the hourly run posts each new correction by itself, a few a run.
- Either way it needs the master switch on and nothing paused (the post types' own switches are for new posts; a correction belongs to a post that already went out), the OneUp key, and Facebook's id for the post — from OneUp's published list, or the daily read of how posts did; until it is known, the correction says so. **Never on a Sunday**, and never with a `[VERIFY]` in it.
- **Never twice.** The try is noted before it is sent. If OneUp's answer never comes back, the plugin looks for the comment among the page's comments (through OneUp) on the next runs: found, it is marked posted; until then nothing is sent again, and if the post is there without it, a person decides with **Post it again** (check the post on Facebook first).
- OneUp refusing it three times stops the automatic tries for that correction (the button still works); a plan without comments is said on the correction, and the hourly run does not try again for a week.
- A later change to the same post drafts a new correction (what is true now against what the post said), which goes the same way.

### Google Business Profile

DJ, Oct 13, 2026: "I want to be able to schedule GBM posts from here." Google Business Profile is the second network the plugin sends to, through the same OneUp category as the Facebook page. Each post that goes to Google is made from the Facebook words, without a Claude call:

- **An "update" post** with a **Learn more** button to the listing (its link tagged `utm_source=gbp`), **one picture** — the post's first; a post whose media is a video link gets the listing's best photo instead, since Google shows a picture, not a link — not also added to the profile's Photos, and **no first comment** (Google has none).
- **No phone numbers**: Google refuses a post with one in it (the profile has its own Call button), so each sentence carrying a number goes, with a short question leading to it ("Questions?"). **No hashtags**. At most **1,500 characters**, cut at a paragraph or sentence.
- **A minute after the Facebook post**, so the two are never the same words at the same minute on OneUp's list (a post whose answer was lost is found there by its words and time).
- **Which posts**: the types ticked under Settings → Google Business Profile (Listing, Open house, Auction week and Results at install), each still needing its own switch on under Safety switches.

**On or off.** One switch, **Google posts**, off at install. On, Google posts go when **all** of these hold: the master switch on, nothing paused, the post's type on and ticked for Google, a location chosen, **Verify for Google** passed for that location (the same safe draft round trip as Verify OneUp, for the location, as an update; set another location, category or key and it must pass again), and the Intel Auctions Toolkit at **4.68.0** or newer (so its own Google post stands down — below). Until Verify has passed, the switches line says "Google: on, not sending yet" and why, and each post's Google tab says it is not sent and why. **Off means off**: switched off (or the master switch, Pause or the post's type), nothing new goes to Google and Google copies on OneUp not yet out come back off — the Facebook posts are not touched.

**Google keeps its own books.** What went to Google — its OneUp post id, the words and time sent — is kept apart from what went to Facebook, and every rule Facebook's pushes keep holds for Google's on its own: one run at a time; nothing new inside the 48-hour veto window unless approved; an answer that never came back is looked for on OneUp's list before anything is sent again; a copy is replaced or taken back only by its OneUp id, and "not found" counts only when OneUp's list agrees; a post that changes is replaced, and one no longer ready (dropped, skipped, cancelled, stopped for a decision, its type unticked for Google, Paused while it changed) is taken back. A cancelled listing's Google copy comes off at once.

- **Refused three times**, or **could not be published** on Google (OneUp's failed list): Google is **off for that post**, with the reason on its Google tab — the Facebook post is not affected. **Send it to Google again** tries once more.
- **A person moved or edited a Google copy in OneUp**: kept as they made it, never replaced (it comes off only if the post is skipped or cancelled, or kept off Google).
- **A person deleted a Google copy in OneUp** (missing from OneUp's whole list on two read-backs at least half an hour apart): Google stays off for that post until someone presses **Send it to Google again**. The Facebook post goes on as planned.
- **Published** on Google: noted on the post.
- **Keep this one off Google** on a post's Google tab takes its Google copy off at once and keeps it off; **Send it to Google again** puts it back (it goes even inside the veto window — that press is the yes).

**Handing Google over from the toolkit.** Until 0.9.0 the toolkit drafted a Google post for each new auction and queued **Review and post: GBP post** for someone to paste, and its social webhook carried Google. This plugin tells the toolkit when it posts Google itself (the toolkit's `ia_social_gbp_managed`, from toolkit 4.68.0): only while Google posts go **and Listing posts go to Google**. From then on the toolkit drafts no Google post and queues no review task (closing any still waiting), its promo cards stop asking for the Google post, and its webhook leaves Google out — and is not called at all when this plugin posts Facebook too. With an older toolkit, nothing is sent to Google, so the profile never gets a listing twice.

### Property videos to YouTube

DJ, Oct 14, 2026: "The goal is to cut down on human workload. I want one time upload, title, description and not to think about it again. I would love to just give you the file and then have you upload to Youtube with description as part of a single property workflow."

**The one thing a person does.** On **Auctions → Add Missing Content → Property Videos** (the toolkit's screen, 4.69.0), each listing still missing a video has **Upload video file** under its link box. Choose the walkthrough (.mp4 — or .mov, .m4v, .webm — up to 4 GB, Facebook's limit) and keep the page open while the bar fills. The file has to reach OneUp's storage (OneUp hands the site a one-time upload address; its Basic plan has none), and it goes one of two ways — **Verify for YouTube** finds out which, and nobody chooses:

- **Through the website** (0.22.2; the Intel account's storage, on DigitalOcean, takes no upload from a browser at all). The browser sends the file to the website in 8 MB pieces (smaller where the website's PHP takes less) — each piece far under Elementor Cloud's and Cloudflare's limits, so a 2 GB file is fine; a dropped piece is sent again and the website says where to carry on. Once the bar is full the page can be closed: the website asks OneUp for an upload address, sends the whole file there, checks it arrived, and deletes its own copy. Meanwhile the website needs room for the file (it says so at once if it has not), and its copy waits in `wp-content/uploads/intel-social/video-tmp/` under a random name. A send that does not get through — or is cut short — is tried again by the hourly run, last of all in it, three tries counted from the moment each starts; then the row says why and asks for the file again. Every piece carries the upload's own key, so a second upload started for the same listing (another tab, another person) never mixes its file with the first.
- **Straight from the browser** (a storage that allows it): the file goes directly to OneUp's storage and the website's disk plays no part.

Then the row says **On its way** and nobody touches it again. Until YouTube is set up (a channel chosen, Verify passed, and the rest under *On or off*), the row says what is missing and links to **Social Settings → YouTube** instead of showing the button:

1. **To YouTube**, a few minutes later, through OneUp, into the playlist chosen in Settings. Its **title** is the address and the sale date ("12 Alpha Road, Narvon, PA | Auction Nov 28, 2026 | Intel Auctions", within YouTube's 100 characters). Its **description** is built from the listing alone — what it is and where, the property's size, the sale day and time, how bidding works when it is by Intel Phone Bidding, the open houses still ahead, the listing link (tagged `utm_source=youtube`), the listing's own description, who to call, a **Subscribe** link to the channel, the office's name, address and phone exactly as the brand brief has them, and three tags. Never the starting bid (it changes, and a YouTube description is not corrected from here). **No Claude call writes any of it**, so nothing can be invented and nobody has to approve it; a listing description with a fair-housing phrase in it is left out of the video's description rather than held.
2. **The link onto the listing.** Once OneUp has published it, the video's YouTube link goes into the listing's video field (the toolkit's `ia_video_save()`: Skip video cleared, the Next Step card asking for a video closed), so the listing shows the YouTube video and people find the channel from the site. The video's id comes from OneUp's published list, else from OneUp's YouTube analytics, else the channel's public feed — matched by its title; nothing is guessed. A link a person put on the listing meanwhile **stays**: the YouTube link is only noted. If the link cannot be found two days on, the listing's history says to paste it on the row. If the listing's video field will not take it, it is tried again on the next checks — the video is not counted as linked until the field keeps it (toolkit 4.70.1 reads it back) — and after three refusals the history says to paste it on the listing by hand.
3. **Facebook gets the real video.** The same file is the video an Open House post carries (§6), and the Listing post's first comment carries the YouTube link.

**On or off.** One switch, **YouTube**, off at install. Files can be uploaded while it is off (the row says they go when it is switched on). A video goes when **all** of these hold: the master switch on, nothing paused, **YouTube** on, a channel chosen, **Verify for YouTube** passed for it (the same safe draft round trip as Verify OneUp — a video post saved as a draft with no file, so an account that refuses video posts fails here rather than on the first real upload — plus OneUp's storage asked, as a browser would ask it, whether a page on this site may send it a file — a PUT with a Content-Type; yes, and a 42-byte test file goes there as the browser will send a video; no, or no answer, and it goes the website's way instead, as a video then will — read back from its public address either way (never posted), and the channel's playlists read), the toolkit at **4.69.0** or newer, the listing **published** (the description links to it; a draft's video waits), and **never on a Sunday** (a Sunday upload goes on Monday, from the first posting window). **Off means off**: switched off (or the master switch, or Pause) before it went out, a video on OneUp's schedule is taken back by its OneUp id — with the master switch off, without reading anything first; even a minute or two past its time, since OneUp can run late — and goes again when it is on. The same holds for a listing that stops being public (unpublished, withdrawn): its video links to it, so it is taken back and goes when the listing is published again. If OneUp answers that the video is no longer scheduled, it may already be out: it is **never** marked to go again, nothing more is asked about it while things stay off, and the next read-back says where it is. Saving the switch, Pause and Resume act at once.

**Never twice.** One run at a time (the push lock). A try is recorded before it is sent; if OneUp's answer is lost, the video is looked for on OneUp's lists by its words and time and kept when found — never sent again by the plugin. Not found half an hour on, the row says so: check the channel, and if it is not there press **Send it again**. The same goes for a video that was on OneUp's schedule and is on none of its lists a day after its time (deleted in OneUp, say). A refusal is tried again a quarter of an hour later (never every minute while the calendar is open); refused three times, or OneUp could not publish it (its failed list, with its reason): **Try again** on the row. While a video is lost, no other file can be uploaded over it — it may be on YouTube already. An upload never finished (the page closed mid-way) is forgotten two hours on, and the row offers the upload again.

**Where it shows.** The Property Videos row (**On its way**, with where it stands, or what went wrong and its button), the auction's **Social posts** box, and the Social log (**video** entries). The hourly run, an open Social Calendar's minute-by-minute check (it runs for YouTube alone too, with Facebook and Google off), and opening Property Videos (at most every two minutes) read back what OneUp did and send what is ready. Should the channel have the same name as the Facebook page, its videos are never taken for a person's Facebook posts.

### Going live

1. Put the OneUp key in **Settings → OneUp** (or wp-config). Turn on the **master switch** with **every post type off**. The plugin may now call OneUp, but with no type on there is nothing to send: the hourly read-back starts, and nothing is written.
2. **Settings → Look up accounts**, choose the category and the Facebook page from the lists, save.
3. **Verify OneUp**: it saves a test **draft** for the page (drafts never publish; it is set a month out; OneUp's answer carries no id, so it is found among the drafts by its words), reads it back, checks that OneUp's time zone matches the site's, deletes it, and reads the scheduled, published and failed lists. The delete is OneUp's documented `deletescheduledpost` (POST, with the post's id). A test draft Verify could not delete never publishes, and is not left for anyone to delete by hand: its id is kept (`ias_oneup_verify_leftovers`) and the next Verify deletes it first, as the step **Earlier test drafts** — as it does any of its own test drafts it finds on OneUp's draft list by their exact words (0.22.2: OneUp lists a post with a title, a YouTube video, as "title~~~description", which 0.22.1 did not read, so its YouTube test draft was left there unnoted). Every step should say **ok**, and the button then shows **passed for this page**. Nothing is written to OneUp for real until it has — for the category, account and key set now; change any of them and it must pass again. If OneUp scheduled the draft as a real post, Verify fails and takes it off at once (if even that fails, it says to delete it in OneUp now). If a step fails, nothing goes live until it is fixed (the plugin's calls to OneUp are named in one filterable list, `ias_oneup_endpoints`).
4. If the toolkit's social webhook is in use (Settings & Data → Automation has a URL), make the **Zapier/Make scenario** behind it post to Facebook only when the payload's `channels` contains `facebook` (below). ## 8. Approval and the weekly review

### Lane A: approve first

The **roundup** and **Results** posts carry hard facts, so they go nowhere until DJ or Jacob says yes (Settings: approvers). As soon as one has a draft that passed its checks, it becomes **Waiting for approval** and each approver gets an email with the full text, the picture, the first comment and two buttons:

- **Approve** — opens a page showing the post with an **Approve this post** button; that click approves it. (Just opening the link changes nothing, so an email security scanner that follows links can't approve anything.) **The first yes wins**: every other link for that post stops working, and the next person to click is told who approved it and when.
- **Edit in WordPress** — opens the post on the Social Calendar (login needed): change the words, then **Save and approve**.

The links are **signed one-time links**: each approver gets their own, random and kept only as a hash, good for one use and for **4 days**. A link that is used, expired, altered, from before the post's words were changed, or from before a fact behind it changed (a corrected price while it waited) **fails safely** — it explains why on a plain page and changes nothing, and the post keeps waiting. Refused links that belong to no approver (altered, or for a post that is gone) are not logged.

A post still waiting gets a **reminder every morning** (from the planning hour, with fresh links) until someone answers. **It never expires.** **No approval email or reminder goes out on Sunday** — Sunday's go on Monday morning.

Approving puts the post on the plan **at once**, at the next free time at least an hour ahead (the approval was the yes, so it doesn't wait out the 48-hour veto window), and it joins the pushes. Changing the words of a waiting or approved post — by Edit, Rewrite, or because the listing changed and the draft was rewritten — needs a fresh yes: the old links stop working and new emails go out. That includes a fact corrected **after** the yes (say, the sale price on Post Auction Results), and a shared post losing one of its sales (one of two same-day listings unpublished): the approval is withdrawn, the post is rewritten with the new facts and sent for approval again, and it cannot be pushed in between — or, if a person had edited it, it stops for that person instead of being rewritten. A post whose draft is being rewritten gets no reminder and cannot be approved until the new version is out.

### The Monday email

Every **Monday at 7:00** (Settings: day and hour; never Sunday) DJ and Jacob get one email: **this week's posts** day by day with their status, **waiting for approval**, anything that **needs a decision** (failed posts included), **corrections to post as a comment**, **new since last week**, **dropped this week** with the reasons, and a button to **open the Social Calendar** — where any post is skipped, moved or changed, reaching OneUp at once (the email no longer links to OneUp). The first one of each month adds **last month's summary**. **Send the weekly email now** under Settings sends it on demand.

### How each post did

The spec: "Every post records when it went out and how it did." Once a day (on the hourly run, 20 hours apart) the plugin asks OneUp's analytics how the page's posts did over the last 31 days, up to yesterday — **reactions, comments, shares, clicks and views** for each — and keeps them on our posts, with the **engagement rate** (reactions, comments, shares and clicks over the views, as OneUp counts it). A post is matched by Facebook's id for it once known, else by its words at its time, as the read-back does; Facebook's id is kept with it from then on. A person's own posts on the page are left out.

- **Where it shows:** on the post's page (**How it did on Facebook**, with when the numbers were read and **See it on Facebook**), in its hover preview on the calendar, and in the monthly summary below.
- **It only reads.** It needs the master switch on, the key and the page, like every read; **How posts did** in Settings turns it off. Nothing is asked when nothing of ours went out in the last month.
- **OneUp's plan:** analytics come with OneUp's Intermediate plan or above. On a plan without them OneUp answers no; Settings says so, and the plugin asks again a week later (or press **Read now**). Numbers already read stay.
- A post's numbers come the day after it goes out; they keep updating for about a month.

### The monthly summary

Which post types and divisions earn their slots: for each, how many posts went out, are still to come, were dropped, skipped, cancelled or failed, and ran late — the **site visits** they brought — and, once read, **how they did on Facebook**: reactions, comments, shares, clicks and views added up, with the engagement over them. Every link a post carries is tagged with its post type (`utm_campaign=social_listing`, …) and the post itself (`utm_content=p<id>`), and when Google Analytics is connected through **Site Kit**, the toolkit reads those visits for the summary. Without Site Kit the summary shows the counts alone and says so. It is at the top of the Log screen (this month and last) and in the first weekly email of each month.

## 9. What it stores

- `{prefix}ias_posts` — one row per post: its listing(s), type, status, priority, window, scheduled time, OneUp post id, content, the images it used, approvals and its history.
- `{prefix}ias_history` — the openers, calls to action, hashtag sets, templates, shapes and images already used, for the no-repeat rules.
- `{prefix}ias_chat` — Talk it over, for 45 days: each message a person sent about a post (with a fingerprint of the post it was about) and each answer Claude gave (with the text, first comment, pictures and alt text it offered, and what would stop them).
- `{prefix}ias_voice` — the log of final posts, for 120 days (starred posts for good; 400 at most): one row per post a person saved or approved or that went out, with Claude's draft, the final words, who wrote or approved them, what they asked for, and its Learn from this and star switches.

## 10. Requirements

PHP 7.4+, WordPress 5.3+ sending email (wp_mail — the approval emails and the Monday email), the Intel Auctions Toolkit 4.66.0+ (4.68.0+ for Google posts to be sent, 4.69.0+ for property videos to YouTube) with its Anthropic key set (drafts and photo grading use the toolkit's Claude engine and monthly cap), GD with FreeType for the pictures it draws (without it, a Results post goes out with each property's plain photo and the roundup with the brochure covers themselves), and a OneUp account with the Facebook page (and, for Google posts, the Google Business Profile location; for property videos, the YouTube channel — and a plan above Basic, which has no uploads) connected and an API key. Optional: Google Analytics connected through Site Kit in the toolkit, for the site visits in the monthly summary. It uses the toolkit's helpers by name; if a toolkit update ever removes one, the plugin stops with the notice rather than failing half way through a run.
